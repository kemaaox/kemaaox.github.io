We study how explicit domain knowledge should shape what a model learns, what it infers, and what it is permitted to believe.

Ontologies, taxonomies, and declarative constraints already encode a great deal of what a domain knows. Our work in the Semantic Computing Research Lab (COUL) treats that knowledge as a working part of a machine learning system rather than as documentation beside it. The current research areas below are ordered by where symbolic structure enters the pipeline: in learning, at inference, and in the management of knowledge that persists. They build on two decades of work on Semantic Web data management, scalable query processing, and blockchain data management, listed under Earlier projects. For papers, see the Publications page.

04

Application domains

Where the methods are grounded and tested

We look for settings where domain structure is already written down by people who are not computer scientists, and where getting it wrong has a cost that can be measured.

Workforce and careers

Occupational taxonomies, skill ontologies, and career trajectories.

Clinical nutrition

Disease staging and dietary requirements, where limits are regulated and hard.

Environmental monitoring

Forest health and pest progression, where early detection is the point.

Autonomous systems

Knowledge that software agents and robots retain and act upon.

Decentralized systems

Validation over evolving graph-structured state.

Process operations

Instrumented settings where machine-held beliefs can be checked against measurement.
